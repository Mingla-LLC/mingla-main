# Mingla Business Journey-Grounded Growth Structure

Date: 2026-06-24

## Core Correction

Do not describe Mingla Business activation as "pages."

"Page" is only one visible output. The real business product is an operator workflow:

Create the business identity, package a real moment, attach commerce or lead capture, distribute it, run the operation, capture proof, and bring people back.

## Current Product Reality

Consumer app:

- In beta through TestFlight.
- Explorer demand should route to the link in bio / beta access path.

Business app:

- Web-first right now until App Store and Play Store verification completes.
- Business CTAs should route operators to the web business onboarding / beta path.

## What Mingla Business Actually Does

Mingla Business is the supply-side operating surface for venues, organizers, experience brands, creators, and partners.

The strategic spine is:

Create.

Sell.

Run.

Grow.

## Business Customer Journey

### 1. Enter

The operator arrives through founder outreach, cold DM, cold email, field visit, local partner intro, creator collaboration, social proof, or business content.

The first ask must be specific:

- Send one flyer.
- Send one menu item.
- Send one slow-night idea.
- Send one recurring event.
- Send one experience or trip concept.
- Let us mirror one upcoming moment.
- Let us set up one small pilot.

### 2. Set Up The Business Identity

The operator creates or claims a business presence.

Possible setup objects:

- Brand profile.
- Venue profile.
- Public brand surface.
- Hours.
- Menu.
- Location.
- Contact details.
- Social links.
- Team access.
- Scanner access.
- Payments/payout readiness where needed.

Success signal:

- The business has a usable Mingla identity that can support an event, offer, experience, trip, venue moment, or partner campaign.

### 3. Choose The Activation Motion

The activation motion depends on ICP.

Event-forward hospitality:

- Slow-night concept.
- Brunch.
- Tasting.
- Live music night.
- Special menu moment.
- Date-night offer.
- Recurring weekly program.

Nightlife / live entertainment:

- Ticketed event.
- Guest list.
- Door scan setup.
- Comp list.
- Walk-up sales logging.
- Scanner team.
- Event blast.

Recurring promoters / community organizers:

- Recurring event series.
- Ticket tiers.
- Share link.
- Buyer audience.
- Follow-up blast.
- Repeat publish loop.

Experience / activity / trip operators:

- Flagship experience.
- Multi-stop experience.
- Multi-day trip.
- Traveler intake.
- Refund policy.
- Payment plan.
- Checkout path.

Creator-hosts / micro-community curators:

- Creator-curated guide.
- Small-capacity event.
- Venue collaboration.
- Community outing.
- Taste-led city collection.

Pop-ups / markets / temporary experiences:

- Pop-up page.
- Vendor or workshop moment.
- Seasonal activation.
- Ticket / RSVP / lead capture.

Partners:

- Co-branded city collection.
- Sponsored weekend collection.
- Campus discovery pilot.
- Tourism hidden-gems campaign.
- Newsletter-to-Mingla planning flow.
- Revenue-share ticketing pilot.

### 4. Configure The Action Path

Depending on the motion, Mingla can support:

- Public brand link.
- Public event link.
- Public trip link.
- Public experience link.
- Checkout link.
- QR code.
- Ticket tiers.
- Free tickets.
- Paid tickets.
- Approval-required tickets.
- Password-protected tickets.
- Waitlist.
- Refund policy.
- Traveler intake.
- Payment plan.
- Guest list.
- Scanner team.
- Buyer contact capture.
- Email or SMS follow-up where enabled and compliant.

Success signal:

- The visitor knows what the business is offering and can take the next action without DM chaos.

### 5. Distribute

The operator shares the activated motion through:

- Instagram bio.
- Instagram story.
- WhatsApp.
- Email.
- QR code at venue.
- Staff share.
- Creator collab.
- Local blog/newsletter.
- Mingla Explorer discovery surfaces where eligible.
- Founder / Mingla social proof.
- Paid traffic only after the message and conversion path are measurable.

Success signal:

- The business actually shares the link, QR, or collection. A live object that nobody shares is not activation.

### 6. Run The Moment

For events and ticketed experiences, Mingla can support:

- Ticket purchase.
- Buyer confirmation.
- QR ticket.
- Guest list.
- Manual lookup.
- QR scan.
- Duplicate-scan handling.
- Wrong-event handling.
- Refund handling.
- Comps.
- Door cash/manual sale logging.
- Scanner invitations.
- Event reconciliation.

Important truth flag:

- Some door payment rails are gated or partial. Do not overclaim full card/NFC door payments unless the current shipped state supports it.

### 7. Grow After The Moment

The value continues after the event or experience.

Mingla can support:

- Buyer audience creation.
- Brand/event buyer lists.
- Email/SMS marketing surfaces where enabled.
- Campaign templates.
- Business blasts.
- Marketing overview.
- Finance reports.
- Revenue, refund, fee, and top-event reporting.
- Repeat publish behavior.

Success signal:

- The operator publishes again, markets to prior buyers, or gets measurable proof that Mingla helped convert attention into action.

## Correct Activation Language

Use:

- Activated business motion.
- Activated supply.
- Publishable business object.
- Operator activation.
- Live business surface.
- Revenue-capable activation.
- Shareable action path.
- Event / offer / trip / experience / venue / campaign activation.

Use "page" only when referring to an actual product page:

- Brand page.
- Event page.
- Trip page.
- Experience page.
- Checkout page.

Do not use "page" as the main strategy metric.

## Better North Star Definition

Weekly Activated Supply.

Definition:

The number of business users each week who create, publish, and share at least one real Mingla activation that can generate a lead, RSVP, ticket, booking, purchase, buyer contact, qualified Explorer action, or partner proof.

Valid activation examples:

- A restaurant publishes and shares a slow-night event.
- A bar publishes a recurring music night with a QR link.
- A promoter publishes an event with tickets and scanner setup.
- A creator publishes a small-capacity outing or curated guide.
- A trip operator publishes a trip with intake and checkout.
- A partner launches a co-branded city collection.
- A venue sets up a public venue profile plus a real campaign or event.

Not enough:

- Account created only.
- Profile created only.
- Draft exists but is not shared.
- A live URL exists but has no action path.
- A partner says yes but no pilot launches.

## Partner Program Ladder

### 1. Free Founding Partner Pilot

Best for:

- Early local proof.
- High-vibe venues.
- Promoters.
- Creators.
- Experience operators.

Offer:

- We help package one real moment and track whether people respond.

Activation:

- One event, offer, experience, trip, or collection live and shared.

### 2. Low-Friction Beta Partnership

Best for:

- Operators who can repeat but need trust first.

Offer:

- Use Mingla for one campaign or recurring series during beta.

Activation:

- At least two published/shared moments or one recurring series.

### 3. Revenue-Share Ticketing / Booking Pilot

Best for:

- Events, promoters, tours, trips, ticketed experiences, nightlife.

Offer:

- Mingla earns when the partner earns.

Activation:

- Checkout path live, buyer/revenue signal tracked, post-event reconciliation captured.

### 4. Sponsored Placement / Featured Local Experience

Best for:

- After Mingla has enough Explorer attention or city-specific proof.

Offer:

- Featured weekend collection, date-night collection, local spotlight, seasonal guide.

Activation:

- Sponsored campaign live, tracked traffic and actions reported.

### 5. Co-Branded Campaign

Best for:

- Local newsletters, city guides, creators, tourism boards, campus orgs.

Offer:

- Turn curated attention into saved plans, tickets, bookings, and Explorer activation.

Activation:

- Co-branded collection, shared distribution, tracking plan.

### 6. Annual / Enterprise / City-Wide Partnership

Best for:

- Tourism boards, universities, festivals, hospitality groups, large recurring operators.

Offer:

- Mingla becomes the discovery, planning, and action layer for a season, city, campus, or event calendar.

Activation:

- Stakeholder plan, campaign calendar, success metrics, repeatable inventory feed.

## Corrected Sprint Structure

### Sprint 1: Ground The Supply Engine

Goal:

- Define activation taxonomy and map ICPs to real Mingla Business motions.

Cards should include:

- Business activation taxonomy.
- ICP-to-motion map.
- Product journey proof checklist.
- Partner program ladder.
- Demo scripts by ICP.
- Outreach message tests.

### Sprint 2: Build The First Concierge Activation Batch

Goal:

- Turn early operators into real live activations.

Cards should include:

- Source and score A-tier prospects.
- Secure assets for first activation.
- Build draft event / offer / trip / experience / venue motion.
- Configure the action path.
- Get operator approval.
- Publish and share.

### Sprint 3: Prove Distribution And Demand

Goal:

- Confirm operators and Mingla can drive traffic to activated supply.

Cards should include:

- Link-in-bio tests.
- QR tests.
- Instagram story tests.
- Creator collab tests.
- Local blog/newsletter tests.
- Explorer content tied to live supply.

### Sprint 4: Capture Operator Proof

Goal:

- Capture proof that the product solves a real business job.

Cards should include:

- First demand signal capture.
- First buyer/lead signal capture.
- Operator quote request.
- Before/after asset.
- Objection bank update.
- Case-study draft.

### Sprint 5: Expand Winning Motions

Goal:

- Double down on the ICP and activation motion that converts fastest.

Cards should include:

- Repeat-publish push.
- Second event/campaign from activated operators.
- Segment-specific landing path.
- Referral ask.
- Partner intro ask.
- Revenue-share pilot where appropriate.

### Sprint 6: Package Partner Programs

Goal:

- Move beyond one-off operator onboarding into repeatable partnerships.

Cards should include:

- Founding partner offer.
- Revenue-share offer.
- Creator/city guide offer.
- Local newsletter offer.
- Campus/community offer.
- Tourism/city org offer.

### Sprint 7: Decide What Scales

Goal:

- Decide which ICPs, messages, channels, and partner motions deserve the next 90 days.

Cards should include:

- Channel review.
- ICP review.
- Message review.
- Activation cost review.
- Revenue signal review.
- Product gap review.
- Next-quarter growth roadmap.

## Gaps In The Prior Structure

### Gap 1: Too Much "Page" Language

The current structure risks making fulfillment sound like a content publishing task.

Correction:

- Track activation objects and operator loops, not generic pages.

### Gap 2: Partner Programs Need Their Own Workstream

Partner programs are different from single-business onboarding.

Correction:

- Add separate partner-program cards for free pilot, revenue share, sponsored placement, co-branded campaign, and annual/city-wide partnership.

### Gap 3: Product Capability Truth Flags Need To Be Attached To Copy

Some capabilities are live, some are partial, and some are gated.

Correction:

- Every outbound and landing-page claim should be tagged as live, partial, gated, or roadmap before it ships.

### Gap 4: Explorer Demand Must Attach To Real Supply

Explorer content should not be generic hype.

Correction:

- Tie Explorer content to live cities, real operators, real events, real plans, and real beta access.

### Gap 5: Activation Needs A Stronger Completion Definition

Published is not enough.

Correction:

- Activation requires publish + share/distribution + action path + proof capture.

## What The Sprints Should Ultimately Prove

By the end of the 90 days, Mingla should know:

- Which business ICP activates fastest.
- Which activation motion gets shared fastest.
- Which message gets replies.
- Which channel creates qualified operator conversations.
- Which operators can generate first buyer, lead, or Explorer action.
- Which partner programs deserve a real offer page and sales motion.
- Which Explorer content is easier because supply exists.
- Which product gaps block revenue or repeat usage.

The business app is not a page builder.

It is a supply activation system for real-life demand.
